TOPIC: Indonesia port landscape for dry-bulk food & feed cargo: port counts, Pelindo/SPMT footprint and dry-bulk throughput, grain import gateways, mechanised vs conventional discharge, rate benchmarks and draft limits

SUMMARY: The widely repeated '636 commercial ports' is a misreading: Kemenhub's RIPN (KP 432/2017) lists 636 ports in total (28 main, 164 collector, 166 regional feeder, 278 local feeder), of which only 102 are commercially operated and 534 are non-commercial; a further 1,394 Tersus/TUKS captive terminals sit outside that count. Post-merger (1 Oct 2021) Pelindo reports 110 ports in 32 provinces, with non-container cargo run by subholding Pelindo Multi Terminal (SPMT: 20 branches + 3 subsidiaries, ~31 non-container terminals, incl. PTP Nonpetikemas' 10 branches). SPMT dry-bulk throughput was 59.08 Mt in 2024 and 30.63 Mt in H1 2026 (+14.7%), but this is all-commodity dry bulk with no published food & feed split; Pelindo group non-container cargo was ~201 Mt in 2024. Mechanised grain discharge (unloader–conveyor–silo) exists at only three common-user berths — Cigading SGT2 (KBS/FKS, CSU 1,300 t/h ≈ 20,000 t/day, -21 m LWS, 200k DWT), Teluk Lamong Surabaya (2×2,000 t/h GSU, 24,000 t/day, 200,000 t storage, -14 m LWS, operated with FKS' NPLog) and Belawan TCK (FKS Solusi Logistik, 850-900 t/h, ~10,300 t/day) — plus two captive flour-mill jetties (Bogasari Tanjung Priok up to 3,200 t/h; Eastern Pearl Makassar pneumatic). The other public gateways handling wheat, SBM, soybean and corn (Tanjung Priok PTP, Ciwandan/Banten, Tanjung Perak Jamrud, Gresik, Tanjung Emas, Panjang, Makassar public berths, Banjarmasin, Pontianak, Palembang, Dumai) still use conventional grab-hopper-truck ('truck lossing') discharge, evidenced by 1,400-3,500 t/ship/day branch productivity (Jamrud 1,814-3,509; Tanjung Emas 2,480-3,098; Belawan 3,517) and by draft limits of -7 to -12 m LWS that cap vessel size. FKS/NPLog's own benchmark is 30,000 MT/day mechanised vs 6,000 t/day conventional (5x) with 15-20% logistics cost savings, while independent Pelindo data imply 3-7x. Strongest defensible deck framing: 'Only 3 of Indonesia's ~14 food & feed dry-bulk gateways have mechanised common-user grain discharge — all three built with FKS Group — and roughly 11 still discharge conventionally at one-third to one-seventh the speed', with a footnote that several of the 11 are classified by absence of any published mechanised facility. Import context: wheat ~12.3-12.6 Mt (2025/26F) and SBM ~5.96 Mt (2025) per USDA/Reuters. Caveat: all sources were read via search snippets because page fetches were blocked in this environment, so each URL should be spot-verified before publication.
